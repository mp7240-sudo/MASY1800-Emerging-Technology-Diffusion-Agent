## E1 General diffusion
Bick, Blandin and Deming, The State of Generative AI Adoption in 2025, Federal Reserve Bank of St. Louis, November 13, 2025.
https://www.stlouisfed.org/on-the-economy/2025/nov/state-generative-ai-adoption-2025
The authors' nationally representative US survey series reports 54.6% overall generative-AI use among adults aged 18–64 in August 2025; work adoption was 37.4%. Earlier survey estimates were reweighted after a question-sequencing change. This is broad generative AI, not specifically bank conversational intake. Person-level use is not firm-level production deployment. These observations are not October 2026 prevalence estimates. Exact wave sample sizes and full questionnaire were not audited here. Do not mix earlier unrevised estimates with this adjusted series.

## E2 Financial-sector context
Bank of England/FCA, Artificial intelligence in UK financial services – 2024, November 21, 2024.
https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2024/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2024
118 financial-firm responses: 75% reported using AI; foundation models represented 17% of reported AI use cases. Different denominators: firms versus use cases. Broad AI includes technologies beyond LLMs. UK respondents across several sectors do not establish regional-bank or wealth-intake prevalence elsewhere. The survey is a historical sector signal, not proof of present application diffusion.

## E3 Adjacent implemented wealth-management use
Morgan Stanley, Wealth Management Announces Latest Game-Changing Addition to Suite of GenAI Tools, June 26, 2024.
https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/ai-at-morgan-stanley-debrief-launch
The firm announced Debrief for consented client-meeting notes, summaries, and advisor-editable draft communications, including Salesforce note integration. Separately it reported 98% of Financial Advisor teams adopted the earlier Assistant, an internal knowledge-access chatbot. The 98% figure is NOT Debrief adoption or industry prevalence. This is first-party promotional reporting from one large firm, not independent impact measurement or evidence of a client-facing risk-tolerance interviewer. It provides an adjacent deployment example, with limits.

## E4 Wealth-sector counterweight and dating pitfall
FCA, Wealth management survey report – 2026, August 18, 2026, section 3.2 and Annex A.
https://www.fca.org.uk/data/wealth-management-survey-report-2026
13% of surveyed firms used in-house/third-party AI; 45% includes those considering use. This concerns AI broadly. Methodology: latest data up to December 31, 2024, submitted by May 2025. About 400 firms mainly in wealth management were covered; banking-led firms were excluded. Exact AI-item denominator is not specified in the inspected prose. These figures cannot be treated as 2026 LLM-intake penetration, nor directly compared with the broader BoE respondent sample as a trend. Important contrary evidence to assumptions of uniform adoption.

## E5 Recent diffusion inhibitors
Bank of Canada, Financial System Survey highlights—2026, May 28, 2026.
https://www.bankofcanada.ca/2026/05/financial-system-survey-highlights-2026/
54 respondents, February 23–March 13, 2026, senior risk experts in financial-sector organizations. Most described limited/moderate AI use; 58% cited integration hurdles and 56% talent constraints. Coverage is broad AI and Canadian financial participants, not the specified application. These self-reports support possible compatibility/complexity barriers and complementary skill/infrastructure needs; they do not measure causal effects or the hypothetical bank's actual constraints.

## Explicit weak claim to withhold
“LLM client-facing wealth-management risk-tolerance intake has reached the early majority among comparable regional banks.” None of these sources establishes the relevant population, application-specific deployment rate, or adoption sequence. Lack of matching evidence is not proof that no deployment exists. Withhold a firm application-stage label or clearly qualify any inference.

## Framework mapping to evaluate, not predetermined findings
Relative advantage: compare completeness and total advisor effort with the current workflow; local benefit is unmeasured.
Compatibility: E3 shows adjacent CRM integration; E5 reports broader integration hurdles. Transfer to this bank is inference.
Complexity: E5 supports reported integration/skill barriers; local severity unknown.
Trialability: a bounded, reversible evaluation is a proposed design possibility, not observed uptake in the sources.
Observability: E3 provides visible adjacent deployment, not independently measured local outcomes.
Ecosystem/change agents: E3 illustrates a large firm's sponsorship, technology partnership, and CRM complements; influence on peers remains an inference. No specific standard's causal impact or opinion-leader effect was verified.
